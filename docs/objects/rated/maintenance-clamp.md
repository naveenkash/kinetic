# Maintenance Clamp

One-line: rated load, structural, 3,500 kg, a 2.4 m wide x 1.8 m tall x 1.2 m deep hydraulic clamp jaw seated on the Stump's Weave face, the Anchor: twenty clamps on the Stump face at r 12 (four per deck, L1 to L5 at Z 6 to 38 on the Rim datum), with two live per round on two different decks, never L5 and never in the west quadrant that the Scaffold Collapse takes.

## Role in play

The clamps are what the Scaffold ties into. Each ring is anchored to the Stump by its four clamps (03 engine notes: structural anchor field keyed to the clamps). Pull one live clamp and its ring's anchor field releases: a whole ring of Scaffold, 48 bays, falls away from the Stump down the outside of the rings below it onto the Rim bowl. The Breaker's answer to a Climber who refuses to touch the floor is to remove the floor around them.

| Harness | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Climber | Perches on clamp jaws between Slingshots. A live clamp with a Breaker on its ring is the cue to go up a level. |
| Scrapper | Cannot move it. Can drop a Breaker's commit with a scaffold board to the back of the head (300 N s, a stagger, which ends a brace). |
| Rigger | Hangs the ring's ladder hatch shut. A Breaker on the Scaffold moves by ladder. |
| Doubler | Overspin from the Maintenance Ring as it passes: 1.3 s above 1,000 N, moving at 1.05 m/s, which keeps the Doubler out of the fall. |
| Breaker | Owner. Climb to the ring, brace on the inner deck, Haul the clamp's release lever along the Stump's tangent. |

## Physics

- Tier: Rated. Mass 3,500 kg. Break-free 1,000 N on the release lever along the tangent (the lever is a 1.2 m arm; the authoring treats the lever pull as the load). The 1,000 N is a commit trigger: a Breaker alone, a Doubler at overspin, or any lines summing to 1,000 N on the lever's axis (a crew, 05 section 6.2). After the 0.6 s commit the authored timeline carries everything regardless of friction or line.
- Commit: 1,000 N sustained 0.6 s within 45 degrees of the lever's arc.
- Release: the clamp's jaws open 0.3 m over 0.8 s. The clamp stays on its tie bars; the ring's anchor field clears; the ring detaches from the Stump and the rings above and below.
- Deck fall: a deck is an 8 m wide annulus from r 16 to 24, about 250 bays at 260 kg, roughly 65,000 kg. Released, its inner edge drops first as the clamps let go in sequence over 1.0 s around the ring from the live clamp, and the deck tips outward and slides down the outer face of the decks below as a sheet, fracturing into bays (800 N s) on the standards it passes. From L3 (Z 22) the sheet reaches the bowl floor (Z -8) in about 2.5 s at 24 m/s. Fragments are budgeted to 60 boards and 80 clamps simulating, the rest baked.
- Players on the deck: airborne when their bay tips past 15 degrees (about 0.4 s after their quadrant's clamp releases). Fall height is the deck's height above the bowl floor: L1 is 14 m (16.6 m/s), L3 is 30 m (24 m/s), L5 is 46 m (30 m/s). Scrapped in every case; the only save is a Hang to the deck above before the tip.
- Decks above the released deck: lose their ladder landings at that level (the caged ladders at r 20 to 23 stand on their own standards and survive with a gap). They do not fall. The Scaffold becomes a tower with a missing floor.
- Players below the ring on the bowl: a 14 m/s sheet of boards; each board 18 kg x 14 m/s = 250 N s (under stagger alone, but ten boards is a flip and a 2 m board pile is a crush pocket against the bowl at 8 kN). Scrap ring on the bowl: 6 m from the sheet's landing line.

The bowl below has a debris field 5 m wide around the Stump: a board pile with a x1.2 wood multiplier and 0.6 m of trip clutter. |

What breaks: the ring's 48 bays, its four tie-bay ties, boards and clamps, the ladder hatch continuity, anything under it. Reset between rounds.

## Interactions

- **Haul** the lever along its arc from a brace on the inner deck: commit. The Breaker is on the ring they are dropping. They have 1.0 s of groan plus 0.25 s times their quadrant's distance from the live clamp (they are at the live clamp, so 0.25 s) to Hang to a clamp on the ring above. That is the Breaker's own panic window and is intentional: the HTR play costs the Breaker their footing unless they rig for it first. HTR crews Hang the Breaker to the ring above before the pull.
- **Jack** off a clamp jaw: the standard Scaffold-to-Stump Slingshot start for the Ascension.
- **Hang** from a clamp: a Hang line never unseats a placed object (05 section 6.2), so a Pendulum from a live clamp's shoulder is safe for any class up to the class Hang load. Hang an enemy to a tie bar before committing: they ride the ring down.
- Chain: the sheet falls across the Maintenance Ring's rail if the Ring is in that quadrant: the Ring is kinematic and shreds the boards (nothing stops it). A sheet over the Well's inner gap: nothing; the ring tips outward by authoring.
