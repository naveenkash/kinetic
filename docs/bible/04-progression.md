# KINETIC — Progression

## Stance: no tension for sale

Everything a player earns is **access, identity, or expression**. Rated power never moves. A Greenhand's Spool pulls exactly as hard as a Wright's. The lore supports this directly: the Accord standardizes harness output, and the Office inspects Spools before every sanctioned match. The shop says so, in the Office's voice.

Progression is therefore about becoming _someone_ on the Lay, not becoming stronger.

## The fiction: the Ledger

Every rigger has a Ledger. The Guild has kept one for every rigger since L.3: tons moved, lines cut, days up, names of the dead. In-game the Ledger is your profile, your career record, and your lore codex, all one screen. It reads like a worn logbook because it is one.

Everything below writes to the Ledger.

## Tonnage (experience)

Experience is called Tonnage and is earned, literally, from mass moved under your lines, multiplied by distance, plus eliminations and assists.

- Dropping a forty-ton drive dog down the Spine in Ballast is worth more than a scrap.
- **Spotter credit:** if your line set up a teammate's kill (the set line they tripped on, the load you hung), you earn the larger share. The Spotter is a real role in a crew and the economy should say so.
- Ring-outs pay by height fallen. The Anchor's Well pays most.

Design intent: reward physics play. A player who never scraps anyone but moves the map all match should out-earn one who only cuts lines.

## Grades (rank)

| Grade | Guild name | HTR | Ascendancy | Hangers | Fray |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greenhand | Probationer | Novice | Kid | Mark |
| 2 | Hand | Operator | Pilgrim | Hanger | Player |
| 3 | Rigger | Technician | Climber | Crew | Earner |
| 4 | Lead | Supervisor | Ascendant | Lead | Book |
| 5 | Wright | Director | Verger | Elder | House |
| 6 | Master Wright | Principal | Exalted | Grandparent | the Fray |

Grade gates sidegrades (Lines, Dogs, Cutters below) and cosmetics, never stats. The jump from Greenhand to Hand is the **Cutter ceremony**: the first time the player's Thermal Knife is theirs rather than a loaner, with a serial number written in the Ledger. This should be a moment.

## The Weight (the free track)

Every rigger starts owing on their harness lease. Riggers call it the Weight. Working it off is the free progression track: every match pays Tonnage against the lease, and reaching zero is a visible milestone on the Ledger ("Clear").

**Sponsorship** is the paid track: someone else carries your Weight and you wear their patch. The game should not moralize about this. The riggers do, quietly, in barks, and that is enough.

## Claims (seasons)

Each season is a Claim on a real section of the Lay, and the five factions compete for it through their players.

- Players declare **Allegiance** to one faction per Claim.
- Every match result feeds a faction's Claim standing. At the season's end the faction that holds the Claim decides the section's fate, and **the maps change**:
  - Hangtown: a Hanger win keeps it standing. An HTR win removes a district: a quarter of the map gone, cables hanging empty, next season.
  - Ballast: an HTR win finishes the cut and next season's Ballast is the Crown and Spine only, open to sky. A Guild win stabilizes it: the Breathing slows, the Shear is removed.
  - The Yard: a Guild win keeps the schedule. An HTR win doubles the Haulers permanently.
  - The Anchor: whoever wins gets their colors on the Rim cranes and their Face on the Crown's broadcast board. Nothing else. Nobody has held it twice.
- Each Claim also surfaces **one piece of evidence about the Question**, as a physical object in a map, recoverable by any player and logged to the Ledger. The five factions read each piece differently. By the end of a year of Claims, the audience knows more than any one faction does.

This is the loop that makes the world a demolition site in fact: **the players demolish it, one season at a time.**

## Allegiance and Contracts

Declaring Allegiance opens that faction's **Contracts**: flavored challenges, three per week, written in the faction's voice.

- Guild: _"Hang a line that someone else uses. Twenty times."_
- HTR: _"Move two hundred tons of rated load."_
- Ascendancy: _"Finish a round without touching a floor."_
- Hangers: _"Scrap someone with an object that weighs less than you do."_
- Fray: _"Snap a line on purpose. Survive it."_

Contracts pay faction livery, faction Lines, Face voice packs (the Face begins addressing you by Grade), and Ledger entries that are that faction's version of the lore. Switching Allegiance mid-Claim keeps what you've earned and resets Contract progress. The Guild remembers.

## The Manifest (seasonal pass)

Framed as a salvage shipping manifest: crates stenciled with what they contain, ticked off as they're earned. Free and Sponsored columns. Contents are drawn entirely from the cosmetic list below plus Ledger entries. No Line, Dog, or Cutter ever lives in the Manifest; those are Grade- and Contract-gated so the sidegrade pool is identical for every player at the same Grade.

## The Spool: Lines, Dogs, Cutters

All sidegrades. Every option trades something. The baseline is always competitive.

### Lines (tuning)

| Line | Gains | Costs | Home faction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Baseline | Baseline | Everyone |
| **Longline** | +30% length | −20% tension | Climber, Rigger |
| **Hawser** | +25% tension | −30% length, slower Dogs | Breaker |
| **Quickline** | +40% Dog speed, faster retether | −15% tension, −10% length | Scrapper |
| **Patient** | Set lines last twice as long | Jack strength −25% | Rigger |
| **Hair-trigger** | Overspin window 0.5 s longer | Snap recoil launches harder | Doubler |

Unlocked by Grade. The last two are Contract rewards from their home faction and usable on any harness.

### Dogs (nodes)

| Dog | Effect | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Holds anything | Baseline |
| **Barb** | Seats in Catch Plates from any angle; harder to shake with movement | Lower max load on structure |
| **Mag** | Higher max load on metal | Won't seat in concrete, wood, or rock |
| **Spike** | Higher max load on concrete, wood, rock | Slides on metal |

The choice is a read on the map. Hangtown is wood and cable; the Yard is steel.

### Cutters

| Cutter | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Standard** | Baseline |
| **Hot** | Warm-up −40%, reach −25% |
| **Long** | Reach +30%, warm-up +30% |

Everything else about a Cutter is cosmetic.

## Rope Marks (mastery)

Per named rig: the Gallows, the Guillotine, the Clothesline, the Slingshot, the Pendulum, the Kiss, the Scatter, the Caboose, the Avalanche, Overboard, the Anchor Drop. Three tiers each:

- **Tape.** A colored wrap on your Line near the Spool. Visible to everyone in the match.
- **Band.** A second wrap and a Ledger entry.
- **Mark.** A title ("Gallows Hand," "Caboose Lead"), a Scrap Card variant, and a bark from your faction's Face when you land it.

The point is that an experienced rigger's Line is readable as a résumé by anyone who gets close enough to see the tape, which is anyone about to be scrapped.

## Cosmetics (expression)

| Slot | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Livery** | Harness plate paint, crew colors, sponsor patches | Silhouette never changes. Weight class must read through any livery. |
| **Tape** | Line wrap pattern | Glow color is team-locked. The shop states the Visibility Clause. |
| **Dogs** | Node shape and impact sound | Within readability. |
| **Cutter** | Handle, blade pattern, cut effect | The snap sound is universal; it is a gameplay cue. |
| **Rig Calls** | Emotes, drawn from real rigging hand signals: _all stop_, _slack_, _take up_, _dog in_, and the rude ones | Ascendancy has prayer gestures. Fray has the bookie's signals. |
| **Drops** | Match entry: lowered by a Hook, sliding a line, stepping off a Hauler, dropped like cargo (Breaker) | The Drop is where a Face's personality lives. |
| **Scrap Cards** | The broadcast card shown to whoever you scrap | Faction templates, Rope Mark variants. |
| **Tallies** | Marks painted on your plates by the Office's prep crew | Earned, not bought. Visible in-match. |
| **Voice** | Face voice packs | Contract rewards. |
| **Reserve livery** | A second livery your crew reserve wears when you respawn | Small, cheap, and it makes the reserve fiction real. |

## Onboarding: the Apprenticeship

The tutorial is a Greenhand apprenticeship under Teodora Quispe at the Guild's training yard, a piece of Collar Four.

1. Dog. Haul. Jack. Hang. Each on a rigging task, not a target.
2. The Gallows, on a dummy. Then on Baskoro, who lets you.
3. **Getting dragged.** Teodora lines you to a departing hauler and tells you to find the line and cut it. The window is real. The first time, you don't make it, and the Stall fires, and she explains what that would have been in a red match. The second time you do.
4. The Cutter ceremony. Your serial goes in the Ledger.
5. First Claim Match in the Yard, against a bot crew wearing HTR orange.

Total: under fifteen minutes. The panic window is taught by fear, once, deliberately.

## Lore delivery

- **Ledger entries.** Short text, unlocked by doing things. Most are three sentences.
- **Office bulletins.** Seasonal. Dry. The Office's humor is the humor of a body that has outlived everyone who founded it.
- **LayCast segments.** Pre-match, thirty seconds, skippable. Where the Faces talk.
- **Barks.** Contextual, faction-voiced, never longer than a Hauler klaxon.
- **The environment.** Notices, chalk, graffiti, evidence objects. The wreck is the archive.

Rule: no cutscene longer than a Hauler cycle.

## Open questions for the team

1. **Hero vs class.** This document assumes class-based Harnesses with Faces as identity. If the team wants hero abilities, the Faces are already differentiated enough to carry it, but the silhouette-readability argument gets harder.
2. **Crew size.** Three is proposed because it matches the trade's Lead/Hand/Spotter structure and keeps physics budgets sane. Six-player crews would double the simultaneous line count.
3. **Lethality framing.** The Stall threshold makes elimination survivable in fiction. The Guild's numbers say otherwise. How dark the tone goes is a studio call; the documents are written so either reading holds.
4. **Cutter on map cables.** Hangtown proposes that any Cutter cuts marked slack cables. If this proves too strong or too confusing, restrict it to the Scrapper harness as a faction affordance.
5. **Overspin.** A self-stagger mechanic in a game without health. The timer is audible and the recoil is telegraphed, but the Doubler needs playtesting before the Fray is committed to it.
6. **Mass tiers and networking.** Five distinct mass values on a line constraint under prediction. The engine team should confirm the Heavy-vs-Light pull resolves identically for both clients before the Harness sheet is final.
