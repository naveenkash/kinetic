# KINETIC — World & Lore

_World bible v0.1. In-world date: L.61 (sixty-one years after the Severance)._

## The pitch in one breath

Sixty-one years ago the only space elevator ever built snapped and fell across the equator. The people who take the wreck apart call themselves riggers. When two crews claim the same piece of it, they settle it the way the trade taught them: with mass, tension, and gravity. Four billion people watch.

## What the lore has to do

Every mechanic in the brief gets a reason to exist. This table is the contract between the fiction and the design. If a feature can't be put on this table, it doesn't belong in the game.

| Mechanic | In-world name | Why it exists in the fiction |
|---|---|---|
| Smart Cable | **the Spool** (device), **the Line** (tether) | A rigger's load-moving tool, descended from the elevator's climber drive. It was built to move wreckage. |
| Node A / Node B | **Dogs** | Rigging grips. "Dog it" = attach. |
| Contract | **Haul** | Hauling a load. |
| Repel | **Jack** | Jacking a load apart, like a hydraulic spreader. |
| Suspend | **Hang** | Setting a fixed rigging line. |
| Glowing tether | **a line that "sings"** | The filament heats under load. The Accord's Visibility Clause makes the glow mandatory: _no dark lines_. |
| Thermal Knife | **the Cutter** | The mandatory safety tool every rigger carries to cut themselves free. |
| No firearms | **the First Rule** | Law (the Accord), culture (the Guild's taboo), and money (nobody pays to watch someone get shot). |
| No health bars; kinetic kills | **the Stall threshold**; being **scrapped** | The Harness decides when an impact would have killed you, floods you with gel, and you're out. |
| Ring-outs | **going down the Well** | The Lay is built over drops. Recovery drones (Hooks) wait in the dark below. |
| Respawn | **crew reserve** | Your crew sends the next rigger down. |
| Players can be tethered | **Catch Plates** | The Accord requires harness armor to present dog-able surfaces. Your armor is designed to be grabbed. |
| Destructible arenas | **active claims** | Every arena is a demolition site mid-demolition. |
| Barrels, charges, cranes | **"it was already there"** | The Accord forbids bringing weapons. Staged demolition material is site property. |

Design rule that falls out of this: **every hazard needs a job.** If a propane tank is on the map, a cutting crew put it there for a reason, and the reason should be legible.

## The world

### The Strand

The Meridian Consortium (four equatorial states and three corporations, Halvard-Teague among them) spent twenty-six years building the Strand: a ribbon of **Meridian Weave** running from an artificial island on the equator to a counterweight beyond geosynchronous orbit. Twelve **Collars**, ring-shaped way-stations the size of small cities, hung along its lower reach. Climbers the size of container ships crawled up and down it on field-coupled grips. For twenty-seven years, space was a train ride.

Weave is a carbon-boron lattice ribbon. In tension it is the strongest thing human beings have made. Hit it with a high-velocity point impact and it unzips like a stocking. Both facts matter.

### The Severance (L.0)

At 04:12 local, the Strand parted somewhere above Collar Seven. Everything above the break was flung outward; the counterweight is still out there, a slow comet. Everything below fell east, because that is the direction the planet turns.

Collar One hit the sea. Collars Two through Seven came down across the mountains of the Cordillera and into the river basin beyond, along a 3,000-kilometer track that people started calling **the Lay** before the dust settled. A hundred and eighty thousand people died in the first hour. The cause remains officially "undetermined." See _The Question_.

### The Ribbon Wars (L.2 – L.9)

The most valuable material on Earth was lying on the ground. Nations, corporations, militias, and the survivors who had worked the Strand all went for it. Firefights over Weave destroyed more Weave than anyone recovered. A single rifle magazine can unzip a kilometer of ribbon.

Out of this came the riggers: Strand maintenance crews and salvage hands who knew how to move the big pieces. They were the only people who could get Weave out of a fallen Collar without destroying it, and the only people who could do it without dying, mostly. They formed the **Lay Guild** in L.3. Its first rule: no firearms on a claim. The reason they gave was Weave. The reason they meant was that a trade that works at height already buries enough of its own.

### The Meridian Accord (L.12)

Exhausted and broke, the Consortium's heirs signed the Accord. Its terms:

- No one owns the Lay.
- Salvage is by **Claim**.
- A contested Claim is settled by **Claim Match** under Guild rules, overseen by the **Office of the Lay**.
- The First Rule became law: _"No combatant shall bring onto a claim any device whose purpose is to injure."_ Tools are permitted. The structure is permitted. Gravity, the Accord notes drily, cannot be excluded.

The Office funds itself by selling broadcast rights. The first televised Claim Match (L.19, Collar Four) drew six hundred million viewers. The biggest sport on Earth was born out of a budget line.

### Today (L.61)

The easy Weave is gone. What remains is locked inside the Collars themselves, the climber hulks, and the Anchor: structural Weave that has to be _un-built_, beam by beam, by people who know which beam is holding what. The Lay is one continuous demolition site, three thousand kilometers long, with cities living in it.

Three competing **Second Strand** projects want the remaining Weave. Whoever gathers enough of it re-opens space. Everyone else stays on the ground forever. That is what the matches are about, under the sponsor logos.

## The trade

### The Spool and the Line

The Strand's climbers gripped the ribbon with field-coupled dogs rather than wheels. After the Severance, Guild engineers shrank the coupling into a wrist-mounted Spool that spins a _live filament_: a line whose tension can be driven positive, negative, or locked. Shoot a Dog into anything. Shoot a second Dog. The line between them will Haul, Jack, or Hang on command.

The line heats under load and glows. Riggers say a loaded line is singing. The Accord's Visibility Clause makes the glow mandatory in sanctioned matches, so that the person on the other end always has a chance to see it. The Fray's illegal "dark line" mod is why unsanctioned matches are lethal.

### The Cutter

A ceramic blade that heats past the filament's melt point in a fifth of a second. Every rigger carries one, by Guild law, since L.3, when the first rigger killed by their own line was dragged off Collar Four by a Haul they couldn't cancel.

The Cutter is a safety tool. Nobody lends their Cutter. You receive it the day you stop being a Greenhand, and the Guild writes the serial number next to your name.

Cutting a line under tension releases everything stored in it. Riggers call it the snap. You hear it across the arena, and you know somebody either lived or didn't.

### The Harness

A rigger's exo-rig. It carries the Spool mount, the Stall Gel impact system, broadcast telemetry, and the Catch Plates.

- **Catch Plates.** By Accord rule, harness armor must present dog-able surfaces. Riggers compare it to a judo gi: the uniform exists so you can be grabbed.
- **Stall threshold.** When telemetry reads an impact that would have been lethal, the gel floods, the harness locks, and you're scrapped: out of the match, waiting for a Hook. The Office publishes a 0.3% fatality rate. The Guild's Ledger says 2%. Riggers don't say either number out loud.
- **Weight classes.** Light (the Climber), Standard (Scrapper, Rigger, Doubler), Heavy (the Breaker). Five harnesses, three weight classes the trade talks about, five masses the physics uses. On a line, mass is destiny. A Heavy on a Haul drags the Light toward them; the Light becomes a projectile. Everyone knows this before they pick a harness.

### The Match

- **Crews of three.** Lead, Hand, Spotter. Traditional titles, not enforced roles. Players grow into them.
- **Scrapped** by impact, by hazard, or by going down the Well.
- **Reserve.** In modes with respawn, your crew sends the next rigger down. (Cosmetic hook: your reserve can wear different livery.)
- **Marshals** watch from the overhead gallery and call the match. **Hooks**, the recovery drones, wait below the lowest floor.
- **Prep crews** stage every arena as a demolition site, not a battlefield. Charges are placed where a cutting crew would place them. Fuel sits where the cranes need it.

Suggested modes, named in the trade's words. Not part of this brief, listed so the maps can be read against them:

- **Claim.** Hold the salvage marker.
- **Scrap.** Last crew standing. No reserve.
- **Haul.** Drag a two-ton Weave spool to your crew's truck using lines only. A payload mode where the payload is pure physics.

## The Question

Who severed the Strand? Each faction's answer defines it.

- **The Guild:** harmonic failure that the Consortium knew about, because the riggers reported it. _"We told them it was singing wrong."_
- **Halvard-Teague:** micrometeoroid. Nobody's fault. _"The universe is a demolition site."_
- **The Ascendancy:** it was cut on purpose, from the ground, with a projectile. A judgment on those who used the Strand for cargo instead of for climbing.
- **The Hangers:** who cares. We live here now.
- **The Fray:** they know exactly who did it, and they'll sell you the footage.

The truth is released one piece per season (see _Progression → Claims_). Rule for the writers' room: **every piece of evidence is a physical object recoverable from a map.** A black box in the Hold of Ballast. A maintenance log in the Yard's control shack. Players find the story in the wreck, because that is where everything else in this world is found.

## Tone and look

- **Industrial-real.** Rope, steel, concrete, equatorial light. Reference: demolition crews, high-steel ironworkers, longshore rigging, a vertical shanty district hung on cable.
- **Broadcast on top.** Racing-series liveries, sponsor kitsch, pirate streams with worse compression. The game's UI is the broadcaster's UI.
- **Gallows humor.** Riggers are funny because the alternative is thinking about it. Barks are short, dry, and never quippy.
- **Sound.** Lines singing. Structures groaning a full second before they go. The snap. The Hooks' rotors below you.
