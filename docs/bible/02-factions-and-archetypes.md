# KINETIC — Factions & Archetypes

## Design stance

- A **faction** is who you fight for. A **Harness** is how you fight. Each faction fields one signature Harness and two **Faces**: named riggers who front the faction on the broadcast.
- **Recommendation: class-based, not hero-based.** Pick a Harness; wear a Face as an identity. The silhouette has to read at fifty meters, because the silhouette tells the enemy how a line will behave with you. A Heavy and a Light pulling on each other is the most important fact in any fight, and it has to be visible before the Dog lands.
- **No archetype adds a weapon.** Differences are mass, line tuning, mobility, and knife handling. Every Harness Hauls, Jacks, and Hangs with the same three inputs.
- **Base kit** that everything below modifies: one active line at a time. Firing a new Dog A releases the previous line, unless the previous line is in Hang, in which case it stays as a **set line** for 8 seconds. One set line, one active line. Any Cutter cuts any line, including set lines.

## Harness sheet

Relative scale, 1 to 5.

| | **Rigger** (Guild) | **Breaker** (HTR) | **Climber** (Ascendancy) | **Scrapper** (Hangers) | **Doubler** (Fray) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mass | 3 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| Line tension | 3 | 5 | 2 | 2 | 4 (6 on overspin) |
| Line length | 4 | 2 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| Dog speed | 3 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| Retether | 3 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| Mobility | 3 | 1 | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Cutter | 4 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| Set lines | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Active lines | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |

Read across a row and the matchups write themselves. A Breaker's Haul on a Climber is a slingshot. A Scrapper's Haul on a Breaker moves the Scrapper. A Rigger wants you to walk into something. A Doubler wants to end it before their own line does.

## 1. The Lay Guild — Harness: RIGGER

**Who.** The riggers' guild, formed in L.3 by the people who were on the Strand when it fell and went back into the wreck the next week. The oldest institution in the Lay. Keeps the Ledger. Wrote the First Rule. Trains every Greenhand in the game, including the ones who go on to fight for someone else.

**Believe.** The Lay belongs to the people who work it. A rigger's job is to take a structure down without taking anyone with it. The Second Strand can wait until the Lay is safe to live in.

**Want.** Weave spent first on stabilizing the Collars people live in. A seat at every table. The Question answered honestly, because they reported the harmonics and were ignored.

**How they fight.** Methodically. A Rigger sets the room before the fight arrives: lines across doorways, loads hung over chokepoints, anchors chosen for what they'll do when a Breaker tries to move them. Then they stand somewhere you have to come through. Guild crews talk constantly and quietly.

**Kit: "Journeyman's Hang."** Two set lines instead of one, and they persist 12 seconds instead of 8. A Rigger's Hang line is stiffer: anything that runs into it staggers longer, and it bears twice the load before it slips. Longest reach outside the Climbers.

**Signature rigs.** The Gallows. The Clothesline. The Guillotine.

**Weakness.** Nothing fast. A Rigger caught moving between set-ups is an ordinary target with ordinary mass, and a Climber who refuses to touch the floor never meets the traps.

**Look.** Worn canvas and brass. Harness plates hand-stamped with Ledger numbers. Tape wraps in guild colors (ochre and black). Their Dogs are blunt and old-pattern. They look like they repair their own equipment because they do.

**Faces.**

- **Teodora Quispe, "Mother Line."** 72. Wright of the Lay Guild. Worked the Ribbon Wars at fifteen, pulling ribbon out of the Cordillera under rifle fire, which is why she carries the First Rule like a grudge and never raises her voice about it. She did not write it; she has buried the people who did. She runs the apprenticeship; every player meets her first. In a match she is slow, unhurried, and nearly impossible to approach without walking into something she hung four seconds ago. Rig: the Gallows. _"The building does the killing. You just introduce them."_ Narrative hook: she knows what the harmonics logs said in L.-1, and she knows who signed off on them.
- **Baskoro Hanif, "Hitch."** 29. Guild Lead. Sets five lines before an enemy sees one, and counts them under his breath on the broadcast feed, which viewers have turned into a drinking game. Precise to the point of ritual; he re-tapes his Spool between rounds. The Guild's best hope of holding Hangtown. _"Three. Four. Mind the door."_ Narrative hook: his younger sister fights for the Fray.

## 2. Halvard-Teague Reclamation — Harness: BREAKER

**Who.** The corporation that ran the Strand's climbers, survived the Severance with its lawyers intact, and now holds more demolition claims than any other body on the Lay. HTR's Second Strand site is already pouring concrete in the Indian Ocean. It needs the Weave. All of it.

**Believe.** The Lay is inventory. Sentiment about the wreck is what people feel when they can't afford to leave it. Efficiency is a kind of mercy.

**Want.** Every Collar on the ground and shipped within twenty years. The Question closed as "micrometeoroid" and never reopened.

**How they fight.** By removing the room. Breakers go for the structure, not the player: pull the column, drop the ceiling, tip the stack, and let the Marshals sort out who was under it. HTR crews are drilled, patient, and happy to trade a slow approach for a certain outcome.

**Kit: "Load Rated."** Breakers can Haul and Jack **rated loads** alone: columns, drive dogs, crane counterweights, anything tagged structural. Anyone else needs a Doubler at overspin or a whole crew pulling on the same axis. When braced, a Breaker cannot be pulled off their feet by any line of lower tension; the puller comes to them instead. Highest line tension in the game.

**Signature rigs.** The Avalanche. The Anchor Drop. The Caboose, with themselves as the train.

**Weakness.** Height. A Breaker in the air is a cargo drop with a heartbeat, and their slow Cutter makes the panic window very short. Everything that makes them immovable on the ground makes them terminal off it. Short line, slow Dogs: they have to commit early.

**Look.** Corporate orange and matte grey. Plates are machined, logoed, replaced every match. The heaviest Spool housing in the league, worn low on the forearm. Their reserve riggers are numbered, not named.

**Faces.**

- **Rook Adeyemi, "Wreckingball."** 41. Ex-Guild, and the Guild has never forgiven him. HTR's star and the heaviest harness in sanctioned play. He believes the Guild is a trade union for a trade that should have ended thirty years ago, and says so on camera. In a match he walks straight at the thing holding the floor up. _"Everything comes down. I just pick the day."_ Narrative hook: he left the Guild the week after a red match nobody talks about.
- **Mei-Lin Haldane, "the Surveyor."** 24. Academy-trained, never worked a real demolition, and doesn't need to. She reads a structure like a spreadsheet and calls the failure before it happens, on comms, for her crew to stand clear. Cold, exact, and the most-clipped Face on the feed because she is almost never wrong. _"That column has nine seconds."_ Narrative hook: her models of the Strand's harmonics match the Guild's, and she hasn't told HTR.

## 3. The Ascendancy — Harness: CLIMBER

**Who.** A faith that grew in the Collars in the decade after the Severance, among people who had lived their whole lives in a structure that went up. The Ascendancy holds that the Strand must rise again from the Meridian Anchor, from the same root, and that the climb itself is prayer. Its champions are called Ascendants.

**Believe.** The Severance was a judgment on those who used the Strand to move cargo instead of people upward. Gravity is the thing to be overcome. The ground is a temporary condition.

**Want.** Every gram of Weave to the Anchor. The Anchor Claim, permanently. The Question answered as what they already know it was: a shot from the ground.

**How they fight.** Above you. Climbers Jack off walls, chain Slingshots, perch, and re-dog mid-air. They never fight where the traps are because they are never where the floor is. An Ascendant crew treats verticality as a moral position and plays it like one.

**Kit: "Updraft."** The highest Jack launch speed in the game (18 m/s against 12 to 14 for the others), which is roughly forty percent farther per launch. They can cling to any surface for 1.5 seconds, and fire Dogs mid-air without losing momentum. Longest line, fastest retether outside the Scrappers. Lightest harness in the game.

**Signature rigs.** The Slingshot. The Pendulum. The Ascension (see _Arenas → the Anchor_).

**Weakness.** Mass 1. Any Haul on a Climber is a slingshot, for them or against them. A Breaker who lands one Dog on a Climber's plate has turned them into a thrown object. Their traps are weak; they win by never being where traps are, and lose the moment they stand still.

**Look.** White wraps, bare plates polished to mirror, lines taped in silver. Minimal Spool, worn high on the wrist like a bracelet. Their Dogs are needle-thin. Ascendants paint the altitude of their highest perch on their chest plate.

**Faces.**

- **Paloma Reyes-Okafor, "Swift."** 31. Ascendant. Holds the broadcast record for a full Anchor match without touching the ground, which the Office measured because viewers demanded it. Fights in silence and prays aloud between rounds, to the discomfort of her sponsors. _"Down is a direction. It isn't a destination."_ Narrative hook: her grandmother was on Collar Seven, the one above the break, and survived the fall. Paloma was raised on that story and speaks of it in the first person, which the Ascendancy calls inherited memory and everyone else calls something less kind.
- **Tendai Mutasa, "the Verger."** 52. The Ascendancy's strategist and the oldest Climber in the league, which should be impossible. Quiet, exact, and famous for Jacking between two enemies to send them in opposite directions off opposite edges. _"Separate. Then rise."_ Narrative hook: he keeps the Ascendancy's evidence for the Question in a place no one else has climbed to.

## 4. The Hangtown Crews — Harness: SCRAPPER

**Who.** The people who actually live in the Lay. Layborn, most of them: a generation that has never known a world without the wreck. They built neighborhoods in and between the Collars, on cable, because the ground was taken. They call themselves Hangers. The Office calls them "unlicensed residents." HTR calls them an obstruction to the claim.

**Believe.** Home is where you hung it. The Second Strand is a thing rich people argue about. Everything in the Lay is useful to somebody, and that somebody should probably be us.

**Want.** To not be demolished. The Hangtown Claim, every season, forever. Failing that, a good price.

**How they fight.** With whatever's nearest. Scrappers chain small traps fast: a barrel into a chain into a cart into you. They re-dog before you've finished reading the first line. They lose every straight pull against a bigger harness and therefore never take one.

**Kit: "Quick Hands."** Fastest Dogs and fastest retether in the game. A Scrapper can **recall** both Dogs instantly, which means a failed line costs nothing and a set line can be picked up and moved. Light props and Medium props under 100 kg (propane cylinders, chain, furniture, carts; not fuel drums) travel thirty percent faster on a Scrapper's line. Fastest Cutter in the league.

**Signature rigs.** The Kiss. The Scatter. Overboard. Anything with a cart in it.

**Weakness.** Short line, low tension. A Scrapper cannot move anything heavy, and a Breaker simply walks through their traps. They have to improvise their way to a kill, every time, in the open.

**Look.** Salvaged plates from four different harness makers, mismatched and painted. Tape in whatever colors were in the bin. Spools modified in ways the Office pretends not to notice. Their Dogs are stubby and loud. They decorate their reserve riggers with their neighborhood's stencil.

**Faces.**

- **Jesús Vilca, "Fuse."** 36. Hangtown's crew lead and, ironically, the best demolitions man of the poorest faction: nobody times a barrel like Chucho. Wry, tired, and furious about the condemnation notices on his district in a way he only shows on the feed when HTR is in the match. _"That was already going to explode. I just moved up the schedule."_ Narrative hook: his grandmother strung the first cable across the ravine that became Hangtown.
- **Dela Santos, "Kite."** 17. Layborn prodigy. Streams her matches on a pirate channel with better commentary than LayCast's. Fastest Cutter anyone has measured; she cuts before the line finishes singing. Reckless in a way that reads as joy. _"Nope."_ (said while cutting). Narrative hook: HTR has offered her a contract four times. The fourth offer is still open.

## 5. The Fray — Harness: DOUBLER

**Who.** The Lay's black market, pirate broadcasters, and red-match circuit, loosely organized around whoever currently runs the book. The Fray smuggles Weave, sells dark-line mods, and fights sanctioned Claims mostly to advertise the unsanctioned ones. Their name is a rope term and they know it.

**Believe.** The Accord is a broadcast contract with a flag on it. Everyone is selling something; the Fray is just honest about the price. Risk is the only thing that makes the feed worth watching.

**Want.** A cut. Of the Weave, of the footage, of the bets. The Question kept open, because an open question has resale value.

**How they fight.** Loud. A Doubler runs two active lines and pushes each past its rated tension, betting they can end the exchange before the line ends them. Fray crews are unpredictable on purpose and treat the Marshals' warnings as a soundtrack.

**Kit: "Overspin."** Two active lines at once, tension split between them. Holding the mode trigger pushes a line past rated tension: it sings louder at one second, and snaps at 1.8 seconds, launching the Doubler backward and staggering them. A snapped line delivers its full stored energy to whatever was on the other end, so a timed snap is also a weapon.

**Signature rigs.** The Kiss, overspun. The Double Gallows. The Snapback (deliberately snapping a line to launch yourself).

**Weakness.** They hurt themselves, on a timer everyone can hear. A Doubler mid-overspin is committed, telegraphed, and about to be somewhere else involuntarily. Two lines at split tension means neither is strong alone.

**Look.** Neon tape on black. Mismatched plates with the serials filed off. Spools visibly modified, heat-scored around the housing. Their Dogs are barbed and illegal in three jurisdictions that no longer exist. Fray riggers wear their red-match count where HTR wears a logo.

**Faces.**

- **Nnamdi Ferreira, "Snap."** 33. Missing two fingers to his own line, which he will tell you about at length. Red-match veteran who came to sanctioned play for the money and stayed for the audience. Laughs on comms. Overspins everything. _"Hear that? That's the good part."_ Narrative hook: he has footage from L.0 that he hasn't sold yet, and he's waiting for the price to be right.
- **Odile Lacroix, "the Auctioneer."** 45. Runs the Fray's book and commentates her own matches live, taking bets mid-fight. The best two-line rigger in the game and the only Face the Office has formally sanctioned for "conduct unbecoming a broadcast." _"Going once."_ Narrative hook: she is the one person who has bought evidence about the Question from all four other factions.

## Named rigs

A shared vocabulary. These are not abilities; they are things players will do with the base kit, and the trade has named them. Names show up in commentary, Rope Marks (mastery), and barks.

| Rig | How | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| **The Gallows** | Hang a line from an overhead anchor to an enemy's Catch Plate, then Haul. They hoist. | Cut fast. Or a teammate Jacks them off the anchor. |
| **The Guillotine** | Hang a load overhead on a set line. When the enemy walks under, line the load to them and Haul. | Look up. Cut the set line with your own Cutter. |
| **The Clothesline** | Hang at chest height across a doorway. A sprinter hits a rigid line: staggered or flipped. | Crouch. Cut it. |
| **The Slingshot** | Dog yourself, Dog a wall, Jack. You launch. Chain for distance. | Predict the landing. A Haul on them mid-flight owns them. |
| **The Pendulum** | Hang yourself from a high anchor, swing, cut at the apex. | Cut their line before the apex. Be somewhere else at the apex. |
| **The Kiss** | Haul two volatile containers into each other. | Jack them apart before they meet. |
| **The Scatter** | Jack two volatile containers apart, into two different crowds. | Don't be a crowd. |
| **The Caboose** | Haul an enemy to something leaving: a rail hauler, a falling section, a swinging load. | Cut. Or line yourself to something heavier than the train. |
| **The Avalanche** | Haul the bottom crate of a stack. The stack comes down the way gravity wanted. | Don't stand near stacks on a Breaker's map. |
| **Overboard** | Jack between the enemy and a solid anchor behind you. They go over the edge. | Hang to something on the way out. |
| **The Anchor Drop** | Line an enemy to a heavy load. Push the load into the Well. | Cut before the load clears the edge. |

## The Office of the Lay (non-playable)

The Accord's administration, and the game's neutral frame.

- **Marshals.** Referees in the overhead gallery. Their voice is the match announcer. They are bored, fair, and have seen everything.
- **Hooks.** Recovery drones. The sound of a Hook's rotors spinning up below you is the sound of a ring-out before it happens.
- **LayCast.** The broadcaster. Its graphics package is the game's HUD. Its sponsors are fictional and slightly embarrassing (Stall-Gel by Nurri; Kessl Harness Leasing; Meridian Mutual, "insuring the Lay since the Lay").
- **The Office's position on the Question:** undetermined. The Office's position on everything is undetermined. That is how it has survived sixty years between five factions that want it gone.
