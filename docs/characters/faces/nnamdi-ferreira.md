# Nnamdi Ferreira "Snap"

- **Faction:** The Fray
- **Harness:** Doubler (130 kg)
- **Age:** 33
- **Pronouns:** he/him
- **Role on the broadcast:** Red-match veteran turned sanctioned draw. Laughs on comms. Overspins everything. The Fray's proof that the audience will watch a man hurt himself on a timer.

## Who they are

Nnamdi Ferreira was born in L.28 in Collar Two's lower market, the son of a Fray fixer who filmed things for money (**PROPOSED**). He fought his first red match at nineteen and his last at thirty, and in between he lost the ring and little fingers of his right hand to his own line, which he will tell you about at length, in detail, with the hand held up. He came to sanctioned play for the money. He stayed because four billion people is a bigger room than any red-match pit, and he likes a room.

He overspins everything. He has made the Snapback, deliberately snapping a line to launch himself, into a travel method. _"Hear that? That's the good part."_ He says it at the octave jump, every time, and the feed cuts to his face every time, and he is always grinning.

What he wants: to be the loudest thing in the arena. What he fears: silence, and specifically the silence on a piece of footage he owns. His father was filming in a Collar Two market at 04:12 on the morning of the Severance. Nnamdi has the file. He has never sold it, and he says he is waiting for the price. The people who know him think he has watched it once.

He was also in the crowd at the Collar Six red match in L.52, when a Guild Hand named Iker Ballesteros died on a dark line and Rook Adeyemi left the Guild the following week. He has that footage too. He has not sold that either. His rival is Jesús Vilca, which is a friendship: two demolitions men who disagree about whether two barrels should be brought together by a clock or by tension. Each lands a Kiss in front of the other whenever they can.

## Signature rig and playstyle

A player wearing Nnamdi plays the Doubler as a committed gambler.

- **The Kiss, overspun.** Dog A and B on two volatiles, Haul, hold. At 1.0 s the octave. He releases at 1.6 s with the tension near 1,100 N and the containers meet at the 25 m/s closing cap. Against Jesús's timed Kiss this is the brute-force version and he knows it.
- **The Snapback.** Self-dog A, Dog B on a wall, Jack, hold to 1.8 s. The snap launches him backward at 10 m/s with a 0.8 s stagger. He uses it as a dodge, a reposition, and once, on the Anchor, as an Ascension. He lands most of them.
- **The Double Gallows.** Both lines on one enemy's plates, overhead anchor on each, Hang both, then hold Haul on both to Reel. Two rods shorten at 1 m/s from two directions; the enemy hoists no faster than on one line but has two lines to cut and a 150° turn between them. He overspins one of them on the way up for the sound.
- **No Kick on a body.** His sanctioned gauntlet carries the Accord interlock: the Kick never fires with a Dog in another player's plate, so his Overboard is an 800 N push and a skid. In the pits his Spool had the interlock filed off. He says that is the Spool that took his fingers (**PROPOSED**), and he says it to Greenhands with the hand held up.
- **Two lines, two modes.** One Hang holding a load, one Haul bringing an enemy under it.
- He hurts himself on a timer everyone can hear. A player wearing him should be launched backward by their own line at least once a round and be fine with it.

## Narrative hooks

- **Arc:** Claim 2, Ballast. The evidence object is the Tenacity flight recorder in the Hold, with 04:12 telemetry. The recorder's timestamp matches the timestamp on his father's market footage to the second. A player who recovers the recorder gets a Ledger entry written as Nnamdi's comms, and it is the only entry in the game in which he does not laugh.
- **The L.0 footage:** a Collar Two market, handheld, 04:12. It shows a sky. What it shows in the sky is a writers' room decision. Odile Lacroix has offered for it three times. He has refused three times, which is the only thing he has ever refused her.
- **The Collar Six footage:** Iker Ballesteros, L.52, a dark line, and Rook Adeyemi's face. He has never sold it, and he does not say why. In Claim 2, Rook and Nnamdi are in the same match for the first time since. Nnamdi does not laugh at him.
- **Odile:** she runs his book. He takes her odds as a dare. She knows this and sets them accordingly.
- **Jesús:** the Kiss argument. A joint LayCast segment, thirty seconds, in which they disagree about barrels and agree about HTR.
- **Evidence objects tied to him:** the Tenacity flight recorder (Ballast); his father's camera lens cap (on his body, not recoverable); a Fray pit poster for the L.52 Collar Six red match, pasted on a Yard control-shack wall, with his name on the undercard (environmental, the Yard).
