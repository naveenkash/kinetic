# KINETIC game project

The Unreal 5.8 project (`Kinetic.uproject`, laid out per [docs/tech/project-structure.md](../docs/tech/project-structure.md)). At its centre is `KineticCore`, the rules of the game in standard C++20 with no engine types. The same sources build two ways:
- as an Unreal module;
- headless, tested with any C++20 compiler.

## The Unreal project

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Kinetic.uproject`, `Source/*.Target.cs` | The project, with the editor and game targets. The dedicated server target needs a source build of the engine, because the Launcher build has no server ([standalone-server-option.md](../docs/tech/standalone-server-option.md)) |
| `Source/KineticCore/KineticCore.Build.cs` | `KineticCore` as a module. It uses no PCH and no unity build, has exceptions on (a bad table throws `FTableError`), and force-includes `Private/Unreal/KineticExports.h`, which exports the library's symbols to other modules without per-function `_API` macros. `Private/Unreal/` is the only engine code in the module, and only Unreal builds it |
| `Source/KineticGameplay/` | The primary game module. It holds:<br>- `UKineticRulesSubsystem`, which loads the rules tables once per engine from `Content/Core/Tables` (a packaged build stages them as loose files, `Config/DefaultGame.ini`).<br>- `AKineticCharacter`, the harness character. A 1.9 m first-person capsule moving by its class's row of `DT_HarnessClass`: run, sprint, jump height, air control, crouch speed and mass. It mantles to the class's mantle height and climbs `AKineticLadder` volumes at the class's ladder speed: press into the rungs to climb, or back over the top of a ladder, facing the wall, to climb down. Other players see its class's generated figure (`/Game/Characters/<Class>`, `make import-characters`), playing Idle, Run, Climb, Fall, Brace or Stagger from what the character is doing, its plate rims, mode ring and shoulder lamp in the crew's tint; without those assets, a grey-box column.<br>- `UKineticSpoolWorld`, every Spool line in the level, solved once per step by KineticCore's rules in this order: Dogs sweep the level and seat (a Catch Plate, a prop, the world); lines engage, switch mode after the class delay and time out; a trial solve decides break-free; Haul and Jack forces go to props as impulses at the seat and to players by footing (braced, skid, airborne, hoisted); Hang and Set lines are solved together over every body and go back to Chaos and character movement as velocities. The Cutter swings through it. The project runs at a fixed 60 Hz, the rules' step.<br>- Hits: the character records every blocking hit while the velocity that made it is intact (landings, `UKineticMovementComponent`'s impacts, props striking it with Chaos's impulse), and each step the Spool world runs KineticCore's Impact Monitor on them. A stagger or flip stuns; a Stall takes the player out and their lines go. In a match they are out until the round resets; in the Walk mode they are back at Spawn A after 3 s. A fall below -6 m (the Well, or off the edge) is a ring-out. Hang and Set lines are solid: walking into one stops you, and at 3 m/s it staggers you, at 6 m/s it flips you over. Surfaces are actor tags (`Surface.Scrap`, `Surface.Wood`).<br>- `AKineticProp`, a generated mesh simulated by Chaos with its spec's mass and placement: loose props simulate, racked and stacked ones wait for an active pull that meets their break-free threshold. A round's reset puts them back where the level placed them. `AKineticLineRenderer` draws the lines by mode and the Dogs in flight.<br>- `AKineticGameMode`, in one of two modes (`?Mode=`, or the `kinetic.Mode` console variable). **Scrap**, the default, is a match (`FKineticScrap`): the player in slot 0 with a Breaker and a Climber, against a Breaker, a Rigger and a Scrapper, on KineticCore's `FScrapMatch` (the reset, the Drop, Live, the clock and its calls, sudden death, best of 5, side swaps, idle backfill). `?Bots=all` (or `kinetic.AllBots 1`) puts a bot in slot 0 too, and the player watches it. **Walk** is one character at Spawn A. Either way the player plays the class `?Class=` names.<br>- `AKineticBotController`, a bot rigger. It presses the same buttons a player does, decided by KineticCore's `Bots.h`: the cut reflex on its own plates first, then `ChooseRig` (a Haul into the scenery the level tags `Kinetic.Anchor`, or a Slingshot off a wall behind it), aimed with lead and drop. It walks the navmesh, which is built when play starts; each ladder is a navigation link, and bots take a ladder one at a time.<br>- `AKineticYardDirector`, the Yard's moving parts and events ([events-and-moving-parts.md](../docs/arenas/the-yard/events-and-moving-parts.md)), run from the round's clock: three Haulers on their timetable with klaxons (they sever lines, hit whoever is in the trench at 12 m/s, carry whoever rides them and sweep the plank bridges), the Gantry's 120 s cycle dropping a 2,800 kg plate into the press (a Breaker's Haul strips it), Shift Change at the mid-match event (the plate released over Marker Flat, the Haulers re-phased, the Gantry working the East Pick Stack), the Lockout curtains in sudden death (they sever, shove and crush), the press ram, trench lamps, and the rated loads: the hull sections slide into a trench and the next train carries them off, a pulled column drops the control shack, the counterweight swings. `AKineticKinematic` is the body each of them moves: Dogs seat in it and ride, and its speed judges its hits.<br>- `UKineticHazardWorld`: volatiles (propane, fuel drums, the gas manifold, demolition charges) that arm on an impact over their trigger speed, hiss, and go off 0.3 s later; blasts judged per player by KineticCore's blast table; fire patches, the gas jet and debris shoves as force volumes on players and loose props. The tells' sounds are generated (`art/audio/build_audio.py`, `make import-audio`).<br>- `AKineticHUD`: the crosshair, class, speed and state, the Spool and hits, and in a match the round, score, clock, phase and the Marshal's calls.<br>- `Private/Tests/`, the `Kinetic.*` automation tests. `Kinetic.Core.*` runs KineticCore inside the engine; `Kinetic.Yard.Play` plays the Yard in PIE and checks the spawn, the run speed, the jump height, that walking off a ladder's top the wrong way round catches the rungs, and that running at the mesa's 8 m edge stops at it. `Kinetic.Yard.Spool` plays the Spool on West Mesa: a Haul brings a chair in until the Dogs meet, a Hang holds the player at its length, the released Hang is a set line the Cutter cuts, and a Jack Kicks the player up at the class's launch speed. `Kinetic.Yard.Hits` checks a 5.9 m/s landing staggers, a 20 m fall Stalls against the world, the player is back at Spawn A after the respawn, and the Well is a ring-out. Those three play the Walk mode. `Kinetic.Yard.Events` checks the e 10 train severs a set line across the south trench, Stalls the player standing in it and sweeps its plank bridges, a dropped propane cylinder's blast Stalls the player beside it, and Shift Change lands the Gantry's plate on Marker Flat and re-phases the Haulers. `Kinetic.Yard.Rated` has a Breaker commit hull section R2 into the north trench and the next train carry it off. `Kinetic.Yard.Figure` checks the figure loads, faces forward and plays the right take. `Kinetic.Shots.Yard` (`make shots`, with rendering) saves views of the Yard to `Saved/Kinetic/Shots`. `Kinetic.Yard.Scrap` plays a match of six bots on shorter rounds: the crews start at their spawns and are held through the reset, lines open at Live, the bots come down off the 8 m Stacks and fire Dogs, a round ends, and the next reset has the sides swapped |
| `Tools/unreal/build_yard_greybox.py` | Builds `L_YRD_Greybox`, the level the project opens on, from [the Yard's layout](../docs/arenas/the-yard/layout.md) and prop manifest. It has:<br>- the floor islands, trenches and pit;<br>- the mesas with their ledges and rungs;<br>- the grate and plank crossings, the press, the portals, rail walks, gantry and masts;<br>- every ladder as a climbable volume and a navigation link, with clear floor at its foot;<br>- the navmesh's bounds, and `Kinetic.Anchor` tags on the scenery bots Haul into;<br>- the volatiles (the drums and cylinders, the gas manifold, the demolition charges), the HTR containers, the plank bridges and press ram tagged for the director, hatches over the mast and portal ladders, and the Yard director itself (which builds the Haulers, the Gantry, the curtains and the rated loads at play);<br>- the generated props where the manifest puts them, and grey placeholders for objects not built yet;<br>- the spawns.<br>Where the layout's numbers collide, it resolves them and logs it ([layout.md, Grey-box findings](../docs/arenas/the-yard/layout.md#grey-box-findings-proposed)) |
| `Tools/unreal/import_props.py` | Imports the generated props from `art/out` ([art/README.md](../art/README.md)). It builds the master materials (`M_KineticProp`, and `M_KineticProp_Masked` for weld-mesh cards), the static meshes with their sockets and UCX collision, the textures (the normal map's green is flipped, since Blender bakes OpenGL) and one material instance per mesh. It fails if a socket is missing or moved, or a mesh has no collision, and writes `Saved/Kinetic/import_report.json` |

Content (`.uasset`, `.umap`) is not committed. Like `art/out`, it is generated by scripts and rebuilt with them.

To play the grey-box, open the project in the editor (it opens on the Yard) and press Play. You play a Scrap match as a Rigger on West Mesa, your crew blue and theirs orange: wait out the reset and the Drop, climb down the rungs (step off beside a ladder's top and you catch onto it; anywhere else a walk stops at an edge whose drop would Stall you, though a jump still goes over), and rig them before they rig you. The controls: WASD and the mouse, Space jumps or mantles, Shift sprints, left click fires Dog A then Dog B (or swings the drawn Cutter), E puts the next Dog in your own chest plate, 1 2 3 pick Haul, Jack and Hang, R releases (a Hang becomes a set line), right mouse held draws the Cutter, C held braces, and Escape frees the mouse cursor (Escape again takes it back). For another class, or the Walk mode, run it standalone with options in the URL:

```bash
"/Users/Shared/Epic Games/UE_5.8/Engine/Binaries/Mac/UnrealEditor.app/Contents/MacOS/UnrealEditor" "$PWD/Kinetic.uproject" "/Game/Arenas/YRD_Yard/L_YRD_Greybox?Class=Breaker?Mode=Scrap" -game -windowed -ResX=1600 -ResY=900
```

`make editor` builds the editor target with Xcode 15.2 to 27.9 (the range in the engine's `Config/Apple/Apple_SDK.json`). `make unreal-test` runs the automation tests headless (`UNREAL_TESTS=Kinetic.Yard.Scrap` runs just those). `make import-props` imports `art/out`, `make import-audio` generates and imports the sound cues, `make import-kit` imports the arena kit and its surfaces ([art/README.md](../art/README.md), Arenas), and `make yard-greybox` then builds the level: the grey boxes, the kit drawn over them, and the night lighting. `make shots` renders views of the Yard to `Saved/Kinetic/Shots` for checking the lighting. `UE_ROOT` overrides the engine location, which defaults to the Launcher's `/Users/Shared/Epic Games/UE_5.8`. The editor's log is in `~/Library/Logs/Unreal Engine/KineticEditor/`.

## The engine-free core

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Content/Core/Tables/*.csv` | The data tables (`DT_HarnessClass`, `DT_CutterVariant`, `DT_ImpactThresholds`, `DT_SurfaceModifiers`, `DT_BreakFree`, `DT_MassTiers`, `DT_Volatiles`, `DT_SpoolGlobals`) in SI units, in the CSV format Unreal imports as data tables |
| `Tools/ci/check_tables.py` | Fails the build if any table value differs from [bible/05](../docs/bible/05-core-mechanics.md), or is not a finite number; 231 values today |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/` | The library: `LineSolver` (Haul, the Kick and Jack push), `HangSolver` (every Hang and Set line solved together, with slip, Reel and the floor), `MassRules` (footing, brace, break-free, rated commit, tiers, force volumes), `Spool` (overspin, Doubler split, snap and cut impulses), `Impact` (stagger, flip, Stall, the Impact Monitor, crush, blasts), `Cutter` (warm-up and the cone and reach cut test), `Tables`, `Units` |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/` (match layer) | `MatchEvents` (the event schema, written as JSON Lines: Dog seats, mode engagements, cuts, Stalls, hits, snaps, rated commits, per-round time and mass moved, round ends, connections, server health), `Credit` (elimination, Spotter and assist credit, and the Scrap Card), `ScrapMatch` (the Scrap round clock and calls, best of 5, side swaps, sudden death, forfeit, leaver, idle and backfill rules), `MatchReport` (facts and the report the backend stores), `Determinism` (per-tick recordings and the first divergent tick), `Json`, `Sha256` |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/Kinematics.h`, `ForceVolumes.h`, `Tells.h` | Kinematics as a function of match time (Hauler timetables with Shift Change, the Gantry cycle, the Ring, the Breathing hull), the Haul sled's ratchet, the client's server-time estimate, the swept test by which a kinematic severs lines; force volume profiles (Updraft, Pulse, water rush, gas jet, fire patch) with 05 6.6's mass scaling and cap; tell minimum leads, the `Tell.LeadTimes` check, the client tell scheduler, the server's lead gates, and mid-match event timing ([kinematics-and-force-volumes.md](../docs/tech/kinematics-and-force-volumes.md) section 4) |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/NetCodec.h`, `NetPriority.h`, `Rewind.h` | Replication encodings (1 cm positions, smallest-three rotations, the 32-byte body and 5-byte line), per-viewer priority, rates and tiers, the per-connection scheduler that sheds tier-3 and then tier-2 bodies but never lines or players, the 20-frame rewind history, server-side cut validation (camera turn clamp, warm-up, swing cycle, reach and cone from the rewound eye), the Dog-hit re-sweep against rewound plates, the Stall grace window, the latency policy and correction statistics ([networking.md](../docs/tech/networking.md) section 12) |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/Bots.h` | What the bot behavior trees decide: the cut reflex on lines in the bot's own plates (backfill bots between 1.0 and 1.6 s, the tutorial's Probationers at 1.4 s), a backfill bot's next rig from the base kit, what bots never do, and the tutorial crew's schedule ([game-modes-and-match-flow.md](../docs/systems/game-modes-and-match-flow.md) section 9.1) |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/PlayerState.h` | The player's state machine (Grounded, Dragged, Airborne, Hoisted, Flipped, Staggered, Perched, LedgeHang, Stalled), which body is simulated and when to hand off, stagger and brace timers, what each state allows, airborne Dog fire, the Stall sequence and the Hooks cue |
| `Source/KineticCore/Public/Kinetic/ReferenceSim.h` | A small point-body simulator used to check the solver against the bible's worked examples and to run the panic-window bench. Production physics stays in Chaos |
| `Tests/KineticCore/` | 218 tests, including section 7.3 of networking.md (the latency validation matrix and the grace window), bible/05 section 4.3 (the panic window), the 71:29 split, the Kick, Overboard and its counter, the Gallows, Anchor Drop, shared Hang loads, chains, 200 random line networks, swing loads (a Bola included), slip and Reel, every 7.1 impulse example, crush, and bit-identical determinism |
| `Tools/report/SampleMatch.cpp`, `Tools/ci/check_report.py` | Play a scripted Scrap match and check its report and raw events as the backend will read them (`api.srv_store_report`) |
| `Tools/determinism/DiffRecordings.cpp` | `build/diff-recordings A B [tolerance_m]`: where two determinism recordings first diverge, for the week 2 and week 5 "record positions, diff" tests |
| `Tools/bench/PanicBench.cpp` | Prints, for every attacker and victim class, when a Haul from 15 m lifts the victim off their feet and when it has dragged them 15 m |

The code follows Unreal's naming (`F` structs, `E` enums, `b` booleans) inside a `Kinetic` namespace and uses no engine types, so it builds as the `KineticCore` module unchanged; `FVec3` maps onto `FVector`. Everything is SI; `Units.h` converts to centimeters at the engine boundary.

## Findings while implementing

These change the design documents; they are recorded in [docs/tech/spool-and-line-implementation.md](../docs/tech/spool-and-line-implementation.md) section 9 and [docs/tech/player-physics.md](../docs/tech/player-physics.md) section 4.

1. The Kick's velocity target must be taken at the end of each step. Taken at the start (as the design pseudocode had it), a fixed 60 Hz step never reaches V inside the 0.3 s window.
2. Haul and Jack must use each endpoint's free inverse mass, and footing, brace and break-free must be decided from that step's force. As designed, a Jack could not start a placed object, vent the manifold or break a brace, and Overboard applied no force.
3. Hang lines must be solved together as one constraint solve with impulses capped at the Hang load. As designed, two lines did not share a load, a swing read half its load, slip depended on the tick rate, a slipping line could push, and a Hang line never acted on a grounded player (the Gallows, Anchor Drop and the Overboard counter did nothing).
4. Slip works as a clutch whose friction is the Hang load. The bible's fixed slip rate cannot be applied: from rest it needs the line to push, and as a cap it needs an infinitely strong line (open item 7a). A line that slips past the end of its filament lets go.
5. The Kick counts toward objects' break-free at T, or a Climber's Kick would start a bolted object and a Lift counterweight (open item 7b).
6. bible/05 disagrees with itself on the slip rate: section 3.2 says 0.5 m per 0.1 s (5 m/s), the tells table in section 11 says 0.3 s per 0.5 m (1.7 m/s). The tables keep section 3.2.
7. Inclusive thresholds need a floating-point tolerance: a 1,000 N pull measures 999.9999999 N on about a third of axes.
8. Hits against the world are judged on speed only; an object bearing down on a player is classified less the weight the player carries (PROPOSED). The crush test sums contact points per body.
